# **1\. About this policy**

## **1.1 Purpose**

This Communications & Email Policy (the "Policy") sets out the rules for sending and receiving electronic communications through the Lintel platform. It covers:

* Communications Techforge Developers Limited ("Lintel," "we," "us," or "our") sends to you about your account, the Service, or our products;

* Communications you, as a Customer or Authorized User, send to End Clients or others through Lintel;

* Engagement tracking on emails sent through the Service;

* Compliance with anti-spam, electronic-communications, and privacy laws;

* Our role in maintaining email deliverability and sender reputation;

* How we enforce these rules.

## **1.2 Status**

This Policy forms part of the Lintel Terms of Service and is incorporated by reference into the Acceptable Use Policy, the Privacy Policy, and the Data Processing Addendum. Capitalised terms used but not defined in this Policy have the meanings given in the Terms or the Privacy Policy.

## **1.3 Who must comply**

This Policy applies to:

* Every Customer with a Lintel account;

* Every Authorized User invited to a Customer account;

* End Clients to the extent they interact with communications generated through Lintel (such as reply behaviour, abuse reporting);

* Lintel itself, in respect of communications we send.

## **1.4 Why this matters**

Email and other electronic communications are a critical channel for real estate operations. Misuse damages End Clients, harms our delivery infrastructure, exposes you to legal liability, and undermines the reputation of every legitimate user of the Service. Strict adherence to this Policy protects everyone.

**Plain English.** Use email and messaging features for legitimate business with people who have agreed to hear from you, or with whom you have a clear existing relationship. Don't spam. Include unsubscribe links in marketing. Don't buy lists or send to scraped addresses. We track engagement (opens, clicks, bounces) and use it to protect deliverability.

# **2\. Definitions**

In this Policy, the following terms have the following meanings:

| Term | Meaning |
| :---- | :---- |
| **"Bounce"** | A failed email delivery. "Hard bounce" indicates a permanent failure (such as invalid address). "Soft bounce" indicates a temporary failure (such as a full mailbox). |
| **"Complaint"** | A recipient's report that a message is spam, typically delivered via the email provider's spam button (which routes a feedback loop notification to us). |
| **"DKIM"** | DomainKeys Identified Mail: a method of signing emails to authenticate the sender's domain. |
| **"DMARC"** | Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance: a policy framework specifying how mail receivers should handle authentication failures. |
| **"End Client"** | An individual whose contact details are stored in the Service by a Customer (such as a tenant, buyer, vendor, guest, or prospect). |
| **"Engagement Tracking"** | Capturing data about how recipients interact with messages, such as opens, clicks, bounces, and complaints. See Section 12\. |
| **"Marketing Communication"** | A communication that promotes products or services, including newsletters, campaigns, listing alerts, and event invitations. |
| **"Operational Communication"** | A communication necessary to operate the Service or a transaction, such as receipts, contract signing invitations, password resets, and booking confirmations. |
| **"Payment Processor"** | Has the meaning given in the Billing & Subscription Agreement. |
| **"Recipient"** | The intended recipient of a communication. |
| **"Sender"** | The party from whose account or identity a communication is sent. In Customer-sent communications, the Sender is the Customer or Authorized User; in Lintel-sent communications, the Sender is Lintel. |
| **"SPF"** | Sender Policy Framework: a mechanism allowing a domain owner to specify which mail servers are authorized to send mail on its behalf. |

# **3\. Categories of communications**

Lintel distinguishes between several categories of communications. The category drives the legal basis required, the content rules, and the compliance obligations.

## **3.1 Operational and transactional**

Communications necessary to deliver the Service or perform a specific transaction. Examples include:

* Account verification emails;

* Password reset emails;

* Two-factor authentication codes;

* Contract signing invitations and reminders;

* Payment receipts;

* Booking confirmations;

* Invoice notifications;

* Notice of changes to terms or pricing;

* Service status and incident notifications.

Operational and transactional communications do not generally require separate marketing consent under the NDPA, GDPR, or PECR — the recipient's relationship with the Sender provides the lawful basis. They must still comply with content and identification requirements.

## **3.2 Marketing**

Communications that promote products, services, properties, or events. Examples include:

* New listing notifications to prospects;

* Newsletters about agency activity;

* Event invitations (open houses, viewings, networking);

* Promotional offers and discounts;

* Re-engagement campaigns;

* Cross-sell or up-sell offers.

Marketing communications require a lawful basis under data protection and anti-spam laws — typically consent, or a soft opt-in for existing customers in some jurisdictions. They must include unsubscribe mechanisms and comply with content rules.

## **3.3 Relational**

Communications that maintain a business relationship without explicitly promoting products or services. Examples include:

* Birthday or anniversary greetings to End Clients;

* Periodic check-ins by agents;

* Holiday greetings.

Relational communications occupy a grey area between operational and marketing. Where they are infrequent, personal in tone, and not commercial in purpose, they may be sent to existing contacts on a legitimate-interest basis. Where they begin to resemble marketing or are sent at scale, marketing rules apply.

## **3.4 Internal**

Communications between Authorized Users within a Customer's workspace, such as internal notes, comments, mentions, and chat. These are not subject to anti-spam rules (they are not unsolicited to external recipients), but they are subject to confidentiality and acceptable use rules.

## **3.5 Mixed-purpose communications**

Communications that combine operational and marketing content (such as a booking confirmation with cross-sell offers) are treated as marketing for the purpose of consent and unsubscribe requirements. To remain operational, communications must be confined to operational content.

# **6\. Lawful basis for messaging**

## **6.1 Applicable laws**

Sending electronic communications is governed by, among other instruments:

* The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA);

* Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) rules on unsolicited electronic messages, sender ID, and consumer protection;

* The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018;

* The GDPR and ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, where recipients are in the EU;

* The UK GDPR and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR), where recipients are in the UK;

* The US CAN-SPAM Act of 2003, where recipients are in the US;

* Canadian Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL), where recipients are in Canada;

* Other national laws applicable to specific recipients.

## **6.2 Lawful bases**

To send a communication, you need a lawful basis under Applicable Law. The principal lawful bases are:

| Basis | When it applies |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Consent** | The recipient has freely, specifically, informedly, and unambiguously agreed to receive the communications. The strongest basis for marketing. |
| **Soft opt-in / existing customer** | Available under PECR and similar laws: where the recipient has previously transacted with you (for similar products or services), and was given a chance to opt out at collection, you may send limited marketing without fresh consent. |
| **Contractual necessity** | The communication is necessary to perform a contract with the recipient (such as booking confirmations, contract signing requests, receipts). |
| **Legal obligation** | The communication is required by law (such as data breach notification, regulatory disclosure). |
| **Legitimate interest** | Your legitimate interest (or that of a third party) outweighs the recipient's interest in privacy. Limited application to marketing; requires balancing test. |
| **Vital interest / public interest** | Rarely applicable in commercial real estate communications. |

## **6.3 Demonstrating consent**

Where you rely on consent, you must be able to demonstrate that the recipient gave it. This means keeping records of:

* When the recipient gave consent (date and time);

* How they gave it (form, online checkbox, in-person sign-up, etc.);

* What they consented to (the specific communications or categories);

* The wording of the consent request they saw.

Lintel records dated consent where consent is captured through our forms or workflows. Where you capture consent outside Lintel (such as on paper or through your own website), you must maintain those records yourself.

## **6.4 No purchased lists**

You must not use the Service to send communications to email addresses, phone numbers, or other contact details that have been:

* Purchased from list brokers or data vendors;

* Scraped from websites, directories, social media, or other sources;

* Collected without consent or another lawful basis;

* Obtained from leaked databases or unauthorized sources.

Purchased and scraped lists have notoriously poor deliverability and high complaint rates. Their use damages everyone's deliverability on our platform and may breach Applicable Law.

## **6.5 Cross-border communications**

Where you send communications to recipients in jurisdictions other than your own, you must comply with the laws of the recipient's jurisdiction as well as your own. Common implications include:

* EU recipients: GDPR consent standards, ePrivacy opt-in for marketing, lead supervisory authority rules;

* UK recipients: similar to EU, with PECR applying;

* US recipients: CAN-SPAM requires accurate sender identification, opt-out within 10 days, no deceptive subject lines;

* Canadian recipients: CASL requires express or implied consent and detailed sender identification.

# **7\. Content requirements**

## **7.1 Required content**

Every communication sent through Lintel must:

* Clearly identify the sender (your agency, your organization, or you personally as an Authorized User);

* Provide a valid postal or physical address for the sender (required by many anti-spam laws and best practice);

* Provide a working method to contact the sender (email or phone);

* Use accurate, non-misleading subject lines that reflect the content;

* Use accurate, non-misleading "from" addresses (no spoofing or impersonation);

* For marketing communications, provide a clear and conspicuous unsubscribe mechanism (Section 9);

* Comply with Applicable Law on disclosures (such as agency registration, license numbers, regulatory disclosures).

## **7.2 Recommended content**

In addition, we recommend that you:

* Use plain English (or the recipient's preferred language);

* Keep messages concise and focused on a single purpose;

* Use a consistent sender identity (consistent "from" name and address across communications);

* Include your agency's branding consistently to build recognition;

* Include a recognisable link to your privacy policy;

* For marketing, include the reason you are emailing the recipient ("You are receiving this because...").

## **7.3 Prohibited content**

Communications must not contain:

* False, misleading, or deceptive content;

* Subject lines designed to mislead about the content of the email;

* Hidden, obscured, or hard-to-find sender identification;

* Forged or falsified headers, routing information, or sender addresses;

* Content that violates the Acceptable Use Policy;

* Content that breaches the rights of third parties (such as defamation, copyright infringement, harassment);

* Content that breaches the laws of the sender's or recipient's jurisdiction (such as restricted financial product marketing without proper authorization);

* Phishing content, attempts to harvest credentials, or attempts to install malware.

## **7.4 Real estate-specific content rules**

Because Lintel is a real estate platform, additional rules apply to property-related content:

* Property descriptions must be accurate and not misleading about size, condition, title, location, fees, or availability;

* Pricing must be current and accurate; do not advertise prices you do not intend to honour;

* Where required by Applicable Law, include your estate agency registration or licence number;

* Disclose your role (owner, agent, broker, or otherwise) where this would reasonably affect the recipient's understanding;

* Comply with any disclosure obligations under the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act and similar consumer-protection regimes.

## **7.5 Personalization**

Where you personalize communications using merge fields (such as recipient first name, property reference, contract details), you must:

* Ensure the data sources are accurate and current;

* Test communications before mass sending to avoid embarrassing merge failures (such as "Hello {{first\_name}}");

* Not use personalization to deceive recipients about how you obtained their data.

# **8\. Prohibited communications**

## **8.1 Unsolicited communications**

You must not use the Service to send unsolicited communications ("spam") of any kind. Spam includes:

* Bulk communications to recipients who have not consented or do not have an existing relationship with you;

* Communications to harvested or purchased lists;

* Communications that the recipient cannot reasonably have anticipated based on their relationship with you;

* Repeated communications after opt-out;

* Communications to addresses that have hard-bounced (Section 13);

* Communications generated through any technique that masks the true origin (such as snowshoe spamming across multiple domains).

## **8.2 Fraudulent and deceptive communications**

You must not use the Service to send:

* Phishing emails or messages designed to steal credentials, payment details, or other sensitive information;

* Communications impersonating banks, government agencies, regulators, courts, or other authoritative bodies;

* Communications impersonating Lintel or any of its staff;

* Communications impersonating other agencies, individuals, or organizations;

* Fake invoices, fake receipts, or fraudulent payment requests;

* Communications offering products or services you do not intend to deliver;

## **8.3 Harassing communications**

You must not use the Service to:

* Threaten, intimidate, harass, or stalk any person;

* Send messages designed to alarm or distress the recipient;

* Defame, disparage, or damage the reputation of any person or organization;

* Engage in unwanted romantic or personal communications;

* Coordinate harassment campaigns against any person.

## **8.4 Illegal communications**

You must not use the Service to send communications that are illegal under Applicable Law, including:

* Communications promoting illegal drugs, weapons, or other illegal goods or services;

* Communications soliciting prostitution or facilitating human trafficking;

* Communications constituting hate speech as defined by Applicable Law;

* Communications constituting incitement to violence;

* Communications constituting electoral interference or unlawful political advertising;

## **8.5 Communications during sensitive events**

You should exercise particular care when sending communications around:

* Major public-safety incidents (such as terrorist attacks, natural disasters);

* Religious observances of particular sensitivity to your audience;

* Periods of public mourning;

* Personal bereavements of which you are aware in respect of individual recipients.

Marketing communications during such periods may, even if technically lawful, be perceived as insensitive and may damage the Sender's reputation. Lintel may, in extreme cases, pause communications platform-wide where this is responsible.

# **9\. Unsubscribe and opt-out**

## **9.1 Right to opt out**

Every recipient of marketing communications has the right to opt out at any time, at no cost, and through a simple mechanism. This right applies under the NDPA, GDPR, PECR, CAN-SPAM, CASL, and similar laws.

## **9.2 Unsubscribe link**

Marketing emails sent through Lintel must include a clear and conspicuous unsubscribe link. The unsubscribe mechanism must:

* Be visible without scrolling on standard desktop and mobile email clients;

* Require only one or two clicks to complete (one-click unsubscribe is preferred for Gmail and Yahoo recipients);

* Not require the recipient to log in, provide additional information, or pay a fee;

* Process the unsubscribe within 10 working days (CAN-SPAM standard) — Lintel typically processes within 24 hours;

* Honour the unsubscribe across all future marketing from the same Sender, not just the specific campaign;

* Be unambiguous (do not present unsubscribe alongside confusing alternatives that would mislead the recipient).

## **9.3 List-unsubscribe headers**

Emails sent through Lintel include standard List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers, which allow major email providers (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook) to offer one-click unsubscribe directly in the inbox. By using Lintel's email features, you authorize the inclusion of these headers.

## **9.4 Granular preferences**

Where you have multiple categories of marketing communications (such as newsletters, listing alerts, and event invitations), the unsubscribe mechanism may offer recipients granular choices, allowing them to opt out of specific categories without opting out of all communications.

## **9.5 Opt-out propagation**

When a recipient unsubscribes from a Sender's marketing communications:

* Lintel records the unsubscribe immediately;

* The recipient is added to the Sender's suppression list;

* Future marketing from that Sender to that recipient is blocked at the platform level;

* Operational and transactional communications (such as receipts) continue, as the unsubscribe does not affect those;

* The unsubscribe is recorded for audit purposes.

## **9.6 Re-opting in**

A previously unsubscribed recipient may opt back in, but only through an affirmative action by the recipient (such as filling out a fresh signup form). The Sender must not contact the recipient to ask them to re-subscribe (that would itself be a new marketing communication, which is prohibited after opt-out).

## **9.7 Hard opt-out**

Some recipients may indicate they want no further communications of any kind, including operational. Where this is technically and legally permissible:

* Lintel suppresses all communications to that recipient from that Sender;

* The Sender is notified;

* The Sender should consider whether the underlying business relationship can continue without communications (often, it cannot).

# **10\. Sender identification and authentication**

## **10.1 The from address**

Emails sent through Lintel must use a "from" address that:

* Belongs to the Sender or a domain the Sender controls;

* Is not deceptive (does not impersonate a third party);

* Is monitored — replies to that address must be received by someone who can respond;

* Reflects a current, valid mailbox.

## **10.2 The reply-to address**

Where a reply-to address differs from the from address (for example, a no-reply address with a separate reply-to mailbox), the reply-to must:

* Be a valid, monitored address;

* Belong to the Sender;

* Not be used to obscure the true Sender's identity.

## **10.3 Display name**

The display name shown to recipients must:

* Honestly identify the Sender;

* Not impersonate or be confusingly similar to a well-known organization, brand, government body, or other person;

* Where used by an Authorized User, identify the user and (where helpful) the agency.

## **10.4 Email authentication**

Modern email deliverability depends on cryptographic authentication. Lintel applies the following at infrastructure level:

* SPF (Sender Policy Framework): publishing authorized sending servers;

* DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail): cryptographic signing of emails;

* DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance): policy specifying how recipients should handle authentication failures;

* BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification), where applicable, to display verified brand logos in compatible email clients.

Where you send from a Lintel-managed domain (such as @app.getlintel.org or a transactional sub-domain we provide), authentication is configured by us. Where you use a custom domain (Section 11), you must configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records correctly. Lintel provides setup instructions and verification tools.

## **10.5 Avoiding spoofing**

You must not:

* Send emails appearing to come from domains you do not control;

* Use display names or from addresses that mimic banks, regulators, courts, or other authoritative bodies;

* Forge headers or routing information;

* Use techniques to evade DKIM or DMARC checks;

* Set up DNS records on domains owned by Lintel to redirect or modify authentication results.

# **11\. Custom sending domains**

## **11.1 Why use a custom domain**

By default, emails sent through the Service may be sent from a Lintel-managed sender domain. Many Customers prefer to send from their own domain (such as @soso-investment.ng) to:

* Build their own sender reputation;

* Reinforce brand identity with recipients;

* Maintain consistency with their wider business communications;

* Avoid recipients confusing their communications with Lintel's.

## **11.2 Setup**

To set up a custom sending domain:

* You request the configuration through your account settings;

* Lintel provides specific DNS records (SPF, DKIM, optionally DMARC) for you to add to your domain;

* You configure the records at your DNS provider;

* Lintel verifies the configuration before enabling sending from the domain;

* Setup typically takes between 15 minutes and 48 hours depending on DNS propagation.

## **11.3 Domain warm-up**

New domains have no sending reputation. When you start sending from a new custom domain:

* Recipient mail providers initially treat the domain with caution;

* Sending volume must be ramped up gradually over 4-8 weeks;

* Lintel may automatically limit your sending volume during warm-up;

* Engagement (opens, clicks) helps build reputation, while complaints and bounces harm it.

## **11.4 Domain reputation**

You are responsible for maintaining the reputation of your custom domain by:

* Sending only to recipients who expect your communications;

* Keeping bounce rates low (Section 13);

* Keeping complaint rates low (Section 13);

* Not sending from the same domain through multiple platforms in ways that conflict with authentication.

## **11.5 Multiple domains**

You may use multiple custom sending domains for different purposes (such as one for marketing, one for transactional), subject to Plan limits. Each must be authenticated separately. We discourage "domain rotation" intended to evade reputation tracking — this is a hallmark of spam and may lead to suspension.

## **11.6 Removal of custom domains**

If you stop using Lintel or remove a custom domain from your account:

* You should remove the related DNS records from your domain;

* Failure to do so does not enable continued sending — Lintel will refuse to send from a domain that is no longer authorized in your account;

* DMARC reports we receive for the domain stop being relevant once the configuration is removed.

# **12\. Email engagement tracking**

**What is captured.** Lintel tracks delivery, opens, clicks, bounces, and complaints for emails sent through the Service. This data is used for operational visibility, deliverability protection, and Customer analytics. Recipients should be informed of tracking through your privacy notice.

## **12.1 What is tracked**

For each email sent through Lintel, we track:

| Event | What it means | How captured |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Sent** | We accepted the email for delivery. | Our sending log. |
| **Delivered** | The recipient's mail server accepted the email. | SMTP response code. |
| **Opened** | The recipient's email client loaded the tracking pixel. | 1×1 pixel image embedded in the email body. |
| **Clicked** | The recipient clicked a link in the email. | Redirect through our tracking server before forwarding to the destination URL. |
| **Bounced** | Delivery failed (hard or soft). | SMTP rejection or DSN message. |
| **Complained** | The recipient marked the email as spam. | Feedback loop from major email providers (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.). |
| **Unsubscribed** | The recipient clicked the unsubscribe link or used list-unsubscribe. | Our unsubscribe handler. |

## **12.2 Why we track**

Tracking serves multiple purposes:

* Operational: confirming delivery of legally significant emails (such as signing requests);

* Deliverability: identifying problematic sending patterns before they escalate;

* Reputation protection: detecting and acting on high bounce or complaint rates;

* Customer analytics: helping Customers understand engagement;

* Compliance: maintaining audit trails for regulatory or contractual purposes.

## **12.3 What you can do with tracking data**

As a Customer, you can:

* See engagement metrics in your dashboard (delivered, opened, clicked, bounced, complained);

* View per-message engagement history for individual recipients;

* Export engagement data for your own analysis;

* Use engagement signals to inform segmentation and re-engagement campaigns;

* Trigger automations based on engagement (such as flagging dormant contacts).

## **12.4 Limits of tracking**

Email tracking is imperfect, particularly for opens:

* Many email clients block remote images by default; opens by those clients are invisible to us;

* Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre-loads images on Apple servers, causing potential false positives;

* Some privacy-focused clients strip tracking pixels entirely;

* Some recipients explicitly disable image loading.

Click tracking is more reliable but still subject to recipient-side modifications and link rewriting by security tools. You should treat engagement metrics as indicators rather than ground truth.

## **12.5 Disabling tracking**

Where appropriate, you can disable tracking for specific emails or campaigns:

* Setting-level: disable engagement tracking for all your outgoing email through account settings;

* Template-level: disable tracking for specific email templates;

* Per-message: disable tracking when sending an individual email.

Disabling tracking does not change the email's content, but Lintel will not record opens or click-through redirects. Bounce and complaint signals remain captured because they are received from recipient providers, not generated by us.

## **12.6 Recipient transparency**

Where you process recipients' personal data (which includes their engagement data), you must inform them of this through your own privacy notice. Recipients have rights to access, correct, and delete this data under Applicable Law. Lintel supports these rights as your data processor (see the DPA).

## **12.7 Data retention**

Engagement data is retained for the periods in our Data Retention Policy:

* Detailed per-message engagement: typically 24 months from send;

* Aggregate engagement metrics: longer, in anonymized form;

* Bounce and complaint records: at least 12 months to inform suppression lists.

# **13\. Bounce, complaint, and abuse handling**

## **13.1 Bounce thresholds**

High bounce rates damage sender reputation. We monitor and act on bounce rates:

| Bounce rate | Action |
| :---- | :---- |
| \< 2% | Normal — no action. |
| 2% – 5% | Warning email; review of your sending practices recommended. |
| 5% – 10% | Throttling of your outgoing email; required review and remediation. |
| \> 10% | Suspension of email sending pending review and remediation. |

Bounce rate is measured over rolling windows and per sending domain.

## **13.2 Hard bounce suppression**

When an email hard-bounces, we automatically:

* Mark the recipient address as undeliverable in our system;

* Suppress future sends to that address until you correct or replace it;

* Notify you of the bounce.

Continuing to attempt sending to known-bad addresses harms deliverability and may indicate poor data hygiene. Lintel automatic suppression protects all customers' reputation.

## **13.3 Soft bounce handling**

Soft bounces (temporary failures, such as full mailboxes) are retried automatically by our infrastructure for a reasonable period. After repeated soft bounces, an address is treated as hard-bounced for suppression purposes.

## **13.4 Complaint thresholds**

Spam complaints are even more damaging than bounces. We monitor and act on complaint rates:

| Complaint rate | Action |
| :---- | :---- |
| \< 0.1% | Normal — no action. |

# **15\. Email reputation and deliverability**

## **15.1 Shared infrastructure**

Most Lintel Customers send email through shared sending infrastructure. This means:

* Lintel actively manages the shared infrastructure to maintain good reputation;

## **15.2 Dedicated IP addresses**

For high-volume senders, dedicated IP addresses may be available (subject to Plan or Order Form). With dedicated IPs:

* Authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) must be configured correctly;

* Lintel provides guidance and monitoring tools.

## **15.3 Best practices for deliverability**

To maximize deliverability of your communications:

* Send only to recipients who expect to hear from you (lawfully obtained, recent consent);

* Keep lists clean — remove hard-bounced addresses, honour unsubscribes promptly, periodically remove dormant recipients;

* Use accurate, non-deceptive subject lines and from addresses;

* Provide content of genuine value to recipients;

* Use email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC);

* Maintain consistent sending volume and timing (sudden spikes look like spam);

* Use a balance of text and images, avoiding image-only emails;

* Avoid spam-trigger words and formatting (excessive capitals, exclamation marks, dollar signs, suspicious links);

* Test emails before sending bulk campaigns.

## **15.4 Deliverability is not guaranteed**

We do not warrant that emails sent through the Service will be delivered to the recipient's inbox. Delivery depends on many factors beyond our control, including:

* The recipient's email provider and its spam-filtering policies;

* The recipient's own settings and filters;

* The reputation of your sending domain and IP;

* The content of the email;

* Internet-wide routing and infrastructure conditions.

# **16\. SMS and instant messaging**

## **16.1 SMS through Lintel**

Where the Service supports SMS sending (whether directly or through an integration), the principles of this Policy apply with appropriate adaptations.

## **16.2 Lawful basis**

SMS marketing in Nigeria is subject to:

* The NCC's rules on unsolicited electronic messages and "do not disturb" (DND) registers;

* Sector-specific rules from the NCC, EFCC, and other bodies;

* The NDPA, which applies generally to communications including SMS.

You must:

* Have explicit prior consent for SMS marketing;

* Respect the NCC DND register where applicable;

* Provide a STOP keyword that opts the recipient out of further messages;

* Comply with carrier and aggregator rules on sender ID and pre-approved sender names.

## **16.3 Time of day**

SMS should be sent at reasonable times for the recipient's local timezone — typically between 8am and 8pm. Night-time SMS marketing causes complaints and may be unlawful in some jurisdictions.

## **16.4 Content**

SMS content requirements:

* Identify the Sender at the start of the message;

* Be concise — most carriers limit messages to 160 characters per segment, with multi-part messages billed accordingly;

* Avoid URL shorteners that look suspicious to filtering systems;

* Include a clear opt-out path.

## **16.5 Operational SMS**

Operational SMS — such as one-time passwords, booking confirmations, signature notifications — does not require marketing consent, but should still:

* Be limited to what is necessary;

* Identify the Sender;

* Be sent at appropriate times;

* Provide a contact point if the recipient receives unexpected operational SMS.

## **16.6 Carrier requirements**

SMS in Nigeria is regulated at carrier level. We work through approved aggregators and require:

* Pre-approval of sender IDs by carriers;

* Compliance with carrier-specific content review processes;

* Adherence to carrier traffic limits and pricing;

* Compliance with any sector-specific approvals (such as financial-services SMS).
